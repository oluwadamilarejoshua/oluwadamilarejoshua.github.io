**Summary.** Do macro-fiscal fundamentals move a currency's level or its risk? Using monthly NGN/USD data over the managed-float era (March 1999 to December 2020), structural-break tests (Zivot-Andrews and Bai-Perron) find no shift in the conditional mean attributable to GDP growth or debt servicing. We therefore enter both variables in the conditional variance equation of a Bayesian EGARCH(1,1)-X model, estimated with the No-U-Turn Sampler in Stan.

Debt servicing significantly raises naira volatility: a one-standard-deviation increase raises conditional variance by about 32% (95% credible interval for the coefficient: [0.163, 0.414]). GDP growth significantly lowers it, by about 13%. A significant leverage effect shows that depreciations raise volatility more than appreciations of the same size, and a volatility half-life of about 2.8 months is consistent with central-bank intervention under a managed float.

**My role.** I conceived the study, assembled the data, built and estimated the Bayesian model in RStan, and wrote the paper as lead author. The project grew out of a GARCH-M analysis I presented at the OAUSTECH International Conference in 2025.
