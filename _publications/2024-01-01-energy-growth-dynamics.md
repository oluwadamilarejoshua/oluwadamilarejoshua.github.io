**Summary.** We study the relationship between economic growth and energy consumption, controlling for income, in Nigeria, DR Congo, Egypt, Algeria, and Gabon from 1980 to 2021. Unit-root and Engle-Granger cointegration tests select a VAR or a vector error-correction model for each country, and short-run, joint, and long-run Granger causality tests establish the direction of causation.

The same relationship takes four structurally different forms across these neighbouring economies. There is no significant causal link in Algeria or Egypt. In Nigeria, energy consumption drives GDP in the short run while GDP drives energy consumption in the long run. In DR Congo, GDP has a long-run effect on energy consumption. Gabon shows bidirectional causality, with deviations from long-run equilibrium corrected at 41.75% per period. Energy and growth policy in the region therefore cannot be one-size-fits-all.

**My role.** I conceived the study, assembled the WDI and US EIA data, carried out all unit-root, cointegration, VAR/VECM, and causality analysis, and wrote the paper as lead author.
